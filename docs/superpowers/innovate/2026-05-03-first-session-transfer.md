# Cross-Domain Transfer: The First Session

**Date:** 2026-05-03
**Technique:** Analogical Transfer
**Domains:** Emergency Medicine, Auto Mechanic, Sommelier, Private Investigator

## Problem

What does the first session look like when someone shows up to an AI-guided OpEx system with their own data and their own problem? They're skeptical (burned before), range from expert to complete novice, and need real value in under 2 hours or they're gone forever.

---

## Domain Solutions

### Emergency Medicine (Triage)

**Reframe:** This is an undifferentiated walk-in — highest-risk encounter because the margin for error in the relationship is zero.

**Protocol:**
1. **ESI Triage (90 sec):** Resource prediction, not diagnosis. Expert = ESI-3 (stable, needs methodical workup). Novice = ESI-2 (high risk if you misread).
2. **Primary Survey (60 sec):** Look at the DATA before asking questions. Its shape, completeness, and pathology tell you resuscitation vs workup.
3. **Dispo-Oriented Workup:** Every test answers "can they go home with something actionable?" Not completeness — disposition.
4. **Discharge Instructions:** What we found. What it means. What to do next. What would bring you back.

**Structural principle:** Value is a disposition decision, not a diagnosis. Orient every action toward the smallest intervention that changes what the patient does tomorrow. Completeness is the enemy of timeliness.

### Auto Mechanic (Diagnostic)

**Reframe:** Walk-in who's been to three other shops. One charged $400 for the wrong fix. One said replace the engine. One handed them printout of codes with no interpretation.

**Protocol:**
1. **Write-Up (5-10 min):** Listen. Three questions: what's it doing, when, how long? Look at prior invoices to eliminate what's been tried.
2. **Test Drive (10-15 min):** Replicate their complaint yourself. The scan tool tells you what the computer thinks; the test drive tells you what's actually happening.
3. **Quick Scan + Visual (15-20 min):** Correlate codes with what you see. Run 3 tests the symptom pattern points to, not every test in the book.
4. **Parking Lot Conversation (5 min):** Explain findings in plain language. Show the bad part. Before turning a wrench.
5. **Quick Win:** Fix one thing they can feel. They drove in with a shudder, they drive out without one.
6. **Repair Order:** Everything documented. Next mechanic picks up where you left off.

**Structural principle:** Demonstrated competence before requested commitment. They came back because last time, they drove out better than they drove in.

### Sommelier (Hospitality)

**Reframe:** Saturday night walk-in. Arms crossed, menu held like a shield. Three previous sommeliers poured overpriced Cab and lectured.

**Protocol:**
1. **Opening Read:** Shut up and observe. What did they order? How are they holding the menu? Confidence level?
2. **Diagnostic Question:** Not "what do you like" but "what disappointed you last time?" Reads palate, price memory, and what others got wrong.
3. **Calibration Pour:** ONE glass. Slightly unexpected but immediately legible. Low-cost commitment from both sides. "I heard you, and this is my answer."
4. **Listen to Response:** If they light up, you're calibrated. If they hesitate, adjust without ego.
5. **The Memory:** Write down what they responded to, not just what they drank. Next visit starts from there.

**Structural principle:** Read before you speak, diagnose from the negative space, prove competence with a single decisive act. The enemy is the tasting menu of everything you offer.

### Private Investigator (Case Intake)

**Reframe:** Hostile intake. Client burned three times. Watching for the same tells: vague promises, impressive jargon, bill with nothing to show.

**Protocol:**
1. **Debrief (15 min):** Ask "what did the last three do, and why did it fail" — maps dead ends AND profiles what "results" means to this client.
2. **Triage Interrogation (10 min):** What changed? When? What did you do when you noticed?
3. **The Live Pull (remaining time):** While client is still sitting there, run one concrete action on their case. Show them something they didn't know. First blood.
4. **Case File (5 min):** Numbered, sourced, handed to client. Even if they hire someone else, the file is useful.

**Structural principle:** When trust is damaged, it cannot be rebuilt with promises — only with demonstrated competence on the client's own material, performed in their presence, producing a result they can independently verify.

## Synthesis

### Convergences (4/4 or 3/4 independent agreement)

**1. "First blood" before full engagement.** All four domains produce a concrete result before asking for deeper commitment. ER runs primary survey before history. Mechanic test-drives before scanning. Sommelier pours one glass before presenting a list. PI runs a live pull while client sits there. This is not "show a demo" — it is performing real work on the real problem and letting the client witness competence.

**2. Diagnose from what failed before.** Mechanic checks prior invoices. Sommelier asks what disappointed you. PI asks what the last three did wrong. Three domains independently concluded the most efficient diagnostic info comes from negative space — the history of failures, not the positive request.

**3. Artifact with standalone value.** ER discharge instructions, mechanic's repair order, PI's case file. Three domains insist client leaves with something useful even if they never come back. Not a sales tool — proof that real work happened.

### Novel Relative to Software Onboarding

1. **Disposition over diagnosis** — Software onboarding helps users understand the system. ER says wrong goal. Goal is: what should the user do differently tomorrow? Get them there.

2. **Sensory proof on client's own material** — Software uses sample data and tours. Every domain here insists on the client's own material producing a result they can feel or verify independently.

3. **Reading confidence level before speaking** — Sommelier's opening read has no software equivalent. Onboarding flows are identical for experts and novices.

4. **Negative-space diagnostic** — "What went wrong before" as primary intake question is absent from software onboarding, which asks what you want to do, not what has already failed.

### Most Fundamental Reframing

Emergency medicine reframes the first session from "impressive demonstration" to "triage that produces an actionable disposition." Trying to be thorough or impressive is the equivalent of running every test in the ER: it delays disposition and does not improve outcomes. The two-hour constraint is not a limitation — it is the correct forcing function.
